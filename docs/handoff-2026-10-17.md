## Open review findings

These come from your Codex reviews and have not been verified or fixed in this round.

- **#49 [P2]** The Speed legend's endpoint labels can overlap at 140% text with dual units (`RidesScreen.java` around 1840). The title-shortening check reserves 56 dp without measuring the labels. It needs a narrow-width, dual-unit, large-text regression case.
- **#50 [P3, optional]** The charging glow keeps animating after the hero has scrolled out of the dashboard viewport, because `onVisibilityAggregated` ignores clipping (`DashboardScreen.java` around 1595).
- **#51 [P2]** `RideAutoStopPolicy` keeps the stationary run across repeated stale motion timestamps. A new zero after a long gap can then bridge unknown motion and arm the policy.
- **#51 [P2]** For auto-stopped rides, `LearnedRangeEstimate` divides whole-journal speed coverage by the trimmed duration. That can push coverage above the 95% admission rule.
- **#52 [P2]** `BackupArchive.referenced()` omits the selected Scooters profiles. A Scooters-only restore therefore offers no per-scooter destination or **Don't restore**.
- **#52 [P2]** A complete backup cannot repair malformed live `profiles.items`. `hasData()` and the planner parse the local profiles first, and the form shows "Wrong password or bad file".
- **#52 [P2]** `tests/run.sh` fails on macOS Bash 3.2 (`backup_tmp[@]: unbound variable` with no `/dev/shm`), which skips the three backup suites.
- **#55 [P2]** A discovery attempt starts its timeout before the read actually leaves the BLE queue. With slow write callbacks, reads that were never sent are reported NO_RESPONSE, and `0x79` polling is suppressed for the connection.
- **#56 [P2]** A status read already in flight when a guided test starts is not counted. Its late reply can satisfy a baseline or post-write confirmation, so the test records **Restoration confirmed** without restoring. This is the most serious finding: fix it before any guided test runs on a real scooter.

All other reviews (#48, #53, #54, #57) report no actionable findings.

## What "supported" means now

- **US UT5 MAX (2538).** This is still the only scooter physically verified in Volt. Its existing control bytes and choices are preserved. The simulated control screens compared with 1.3.1 retain their layout apart from the new **More → Compatibility** row. That comparison does not cover every section: App preferences adds End parked rides, backup entry points change, and Developer options adds the model selector.
- **Identified standard NAVEE models.** These get the lock and readings, plus controls mapped from the official app's 2.1.9 rules, with model, region, firmware and fifth-digit gates. Each mapped control shows **Untested here**. Maximum speed offers only the official app's fixed per-model table. Nothing is swept, probed or trialled.
- **Unidentified models.** These get the shared lock contract, readings and an **Identify** action, and every other control stays hidden. The N65i mapping remains unknown, deliberately.
- **Golf carts, e-bikes and other transports** are refused: no scooter commands are sent.
- **Evidence rules.** An ACK, a zero or absent field, or a packet length is never treated as proof of support. Every write goes through `MutationGate`, and `BleController.send()` drops any frame that is not a read, authentication or the exact admitted mutation.

## Hardware checks only you can do

Do these with the scooter parked and you present. Never use test runners against your own phone, and do not clear or reinstall your release install. The contributor-signed builds cannot update it anyway.

1. **Identify on your UT5 MAX** (once #54 is in a build you install). Volt assumes the model ID from serial characters 1–4 (2538), but the official app reads it from the Bluetooth advertisement, often as 5 digits. One capture settles it. If it names a different standard scooter model, D1 keeps your scooter on the UT5 MAX layer and shows "Model ID mismatch". An excluded transport/product ID instead remains unsupported. Please report the value.
2. **Compatibility check on the UT5 MAX** (#55, after its P2 fix). Confirm it only reads: nine known requests, at most 30 s, and no setting changes. Confirm normal polling is unchanged afterwards. Preview the report and check that it contains no serial, address, PIN, names or routes.
3. **Guided units test on the UT5 MAX** (#56, only after its P2 fix). Units → the other unit → approve → confirm the dashboard changed → Volt sets it back. Then repeat with Bluetooth turned off mid-test. On reconnect, Volt must show **Unfinished test** and send nothing until you tap **Restore**.
4. **Legacy Battery widget on the Samsung** (#50) at 1, 5, 14, 99 and 100%: no fill outside the rounded border.
5. **Auto-stop on a real park** (#51, after its P2 fixes). Ride, park, then walk away with the phone. Volt ends the ride only after fresh stationary readings, a lost link and 10 s of continuous disconnect. Note how long it actually takes, and confirm a reconnect inside the grace period keeps the ride.
6. **Speed colours on a real recorded ride** (#49), and the homepage in Samsung Internet (#48).

**The next useful model test** is any standard NAVEE scooter other than the UT5 MAX. Pair it with the usual owner setup, tap **Identify**, then **More → Compatibility → Run check → Preview report → Save file**, and share the report. With your approval, follow up with a guided units test. That would be Volt's first evidence on another model.
